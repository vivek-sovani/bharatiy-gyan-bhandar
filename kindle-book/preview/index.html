Preface

The Living Tree

How Indian knowledge grew from the Vedas into the classical corpus

A library can show you what a civilization knew. It is harder to show how it came to know it — and harder still to convey the most interesting thing of all: that none of this was planned. No committee sat down to design Āyurveda, or grammar, or algebra, or the philosophy of the self. Each grew out of the one before it, often as a by-product of solving a much smaller, more practical problem.

This is a map of that branching. Read it, and the chapters that follow stop being a list of unfamiliar Sanskrit titles and become what they actually are: the record of a three-thousand-year conversation that human beings were having with the cosmos, with each other, and with themselves.

"The four Vedas are not four books but four uses of one impulse — knowing, doing, singing, healing."

The Six Vedāṅgas

The six limbs without which the Veda cannot be read — phonetics, ritual, grammar, etymology, prosody, astronomy. Every limb has its own sūtra literature; together they make the Veda intelligible.

Vyākaraṇa व्याकरण

The face — grammar
Pāṇini · Aṣṭādhyāyī3,959 sūtras

A complete formal grammar of Sanskrit. Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī derives every Sanskrit word from rules — twenty-five centuries before modern formal grammar, and at finer resolution than any of its successors.

Generative grammar — a closed rule-system, not a list of exceptions.
Pāṇinian model — the template every later Sanskrit grammarian works within.
