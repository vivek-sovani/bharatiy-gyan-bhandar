## 01. The Four Vedas — चत्वारि वेदाः
`vedas` · Śruti · foundational · 4 saṃhitās

Ṛg, Yajur, Sāma and Atharva — the oldest surviving stratum of Indic thought, preserved as living sound long before they were written.

*c. 1500–600 BCE · Saṃhitā · Brāhmaṇa · Āraṇyaka · Oral transmission*

### Detail content

#### Ṛgveda

##### Śākhās (recensions)

###### Śākala Saṃhitā — शाकल संहिता

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Structure:** 10 Maṇḍalas, 1,028 Sūktas, 10,552 verses · **Region:** Prevalent throughout India

The standard, widely chanted recension of the Rigveda. Features the unique oral preservation with exact pitch accents (Udātta, Anudātta, Svarita).

###### Bāṣkala Saṃhitā — बाष्कल संहिता

**Status:** Partially Extant · **Structure:** 10 Maṇḍalas, 1,025 Sūktas, includes Khilani/Valakhilya · **Region:** Historically North & West India

Preserves 8 additional hymns (Vālakhilya) and other supplementary texts. Closely related to the Shakala tradition but with small textual differences.

###### Āśvalāyana Saṃhitā — आश्वलायन संहिता

**Status:** Lost Saṃhitā (Ritual texts extant) · **Structure:** Auxiliary ritual works (Shrauta & Grihya Sutras) survive · **Region:** Historically popular in Maharashtra & South India

The independent Samhita is lost, but the ritual manuals (Sutras) written by sage Ashvalayana (disciple of Shaunaka) are the primary authority for Rigvedic rites in many parts of India.

###### Śāṅkhāyana / Kauṣītaki — शाङ्खायन / कौषीतकी

**Status:** Lost Saṃhitā (Brahmana & Upanishad extant) · **Region:** Historically Gujarat & Rajasthan

While the main mantra collection is lost, its Brahmana, Aranyaka, and Upanishad are fully preserved and form an important intellectual lineage of Rigvedic ritualism.

###### Māṇḍukāyana — माण्डूकायन

**Status:** Lost · **Structure:** None surviving · **Region:** Historically Northern India

Mentioned by ancient grammarians and lexicographers like Shaunaka in the Rigveda Pratisakhya, but no text or oral tradition survives today.

##### Core teachings

###### Puruṣa Sūkta

**Citation:** RV 10.90

The foundational hymn on the cosmic being (Puruṣa), describing the unity of all creation and the spiritual sacrifice that generated the universe.

> सहस्रशीर्षा पुरुषः सहस्राक्षः सहस्रपात् ।
> स भूमिं विश्वतो वृत्वात्यतिष्ठद्दशाङ्गुलम् ॥
> “sahasraśīrṣā puruṣaḥ sahasrākṣaḥ sahasrapāt |
sa bhūmiṃ viśvato vṛtvātyatiṣṭhaddaśāṅgulam ||” — RV 10.90.1

###### Nāsadīya Sūkta

**Citation:** RV 10.129

The famous Hymn of Creation. A profound philosophical inquiry into what existed before creation, touching upon the limits of human knowledge.

> नासदासीन्नो सदासीत्तदानीं नासीद्रजो नो व्योमा परो यत् ।
> किमावरीवः कुह कस्य शर्मन्नम्भः किमासीद्गहनं गभीरम् ॥
> “nāsadāsīnno sadāsīttadānīṃ nāsīdrajo no vyomā paro yat |
kimāvarīvaḥ kuha kasya śarmannambhaḥ kimāsīdgahanaṃ gabhīram ||” — RV 10.129.1

###### Saṃjñāna Sūkta

**Citation:** RV 10.191

The final hymn of the Rigveda. A beautiful, powerful invocation for social harmony, unified purpose, common counsel, and mutual understanding.

> सङ्गच्छध्वं संवदध्वं सं वो मनांसि जानताम् ।
> देवा भागं यथा पूर्वे सञ्जानाना उपासते ॥
> “saṅgacchadhvaṃ saṃvadadhvaṃ saṃ vo manāṃsi jānatām |
devā bhāgaṃ yathā pūrve sañjānānā upāsate ||” — RV 10.191.2

###### Gayatri Mantra

**Citation:** RV 3.62.10

The highly revered hymn dedicated to Savitr (the sun/creative power), praying for the illumination of our intellect.

> तत्सवितुर्वरेण्यं भर्गो देवस्य धीमहि ।
> धियो यो नः प्रचोदयात् ॥
> “tatsaviturvareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi |
dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt ||” — RV 3.62.10

##### Brāhmaṇas

###### Aitareya Brāhmaṇa — ऐतरेय ब्राह्मण

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Hotṛ (Recitant) · **Structure:** 40 Chapters, 8 Books (Pañcikās)

Focuses heavily on the soma sacrifice, the fire altar, and the grand royal consecration rites (Rājasūya). Details the priestly roles of the Hotri and contains the moral allegory of Sunahshepa.

###### Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa (Śāṅkhāyana) — कौषीतकी ब्राह्मण (शाङ्खायन)

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Hotṛ (Recitant) · **Structure:** 30 Chapters

Focuses on the daily fire ritual (Agnihotra), seasonal sacrifices, and domestic offerings. Explains the mystical and spiritual rationale behind each external gesture.

##### Brāhmaṇa teachings

###### Legend of Śunaḥśepa

**Citation:** AB 7.13-18

A moral legend highlighting absolute devotion to Varuna and the devas. Demonstrates that sincere prayer and chanting are superior to physical sacrifices.

> ऋचां त्वचमुपमञ्जरीं मन्यन्त एते समाम्नायाः ।
> तस्माच्छ्रेष्ठतमो लोके पुरुषः सत्यमुच्यते ॥
> “ṛcāṃ tvacamupamañjarīṃ manyante ete samāmnāyāḥ |
tasmācchreṣṭhatamo loke puruṣaḥ satyamucyate ||” — AB 7.15

###### Royal Consecration (Rājasūya)

**Citation:** AB 7.1-8.4

The elaborate ritual for consecrating a king. Details the 16-step ceremony where the monarch is ritually reborn and empowered by cosmic forces, establishing the divine right to rule.

> अभिषिच्यतेऽयं राजा सोमेन साम्राज्येन ।
> स देवानां प्रियो भवति ॥
> “abhiṣicyate'yaṃ rājā somena sāmrājyena |
sa devānāṃ priyo bhavati ||” — AB 8.4

###### The Three Fires (Agnitraya)

**Citation:** AB 1.1

Explains the cosmic correspondence of the three ritual fires — Gārhapatya (household), Āhavanīya (offering), and Dakṣiṇa (southern/ancestral) — with earth, heaven, and atmosphere respectively.

> गार्हपत्यो वै पृथिवी आहवनीयो द्यौः दक्षिणाग्निरन्तरिक्षम् ।
> “gārhapatyo vai pṛthivī āhavanīyo dyauḥ dakṣiṇāgnirantarikṣam |” — AB 1.1

###### Agnihotra as Inner Worship

**Citation:** KB 2.5

The Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa's teaching that the daily fire ritual (Agnihotra) is not merely an external act but a meditation on the vital breath (Prāṇa) and the cosmic rhythms of sunrise and sunset.

> प्राणो वा अग्निहोत्रम् । प्राणे हूयमाने सर्वाणि भूतानि हूयन्ते ।
> “prāṇo vā agnihotram | prāṇe hūyamāne sarvāṇi bhūtāni hūyante |” — KB 2.5

##### Āraṇyakas

###### Aitareya Āraṇyaka — ऐतरेय आरण्यक

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Hotṛ (Forest Meditator) · **Structure:** 5 Aranyakas (Books)

Discusses the symbolic meaning of the Mahāvrata ritual. Transitions from external fire sacrifices to internal meditation on the vital breath (Prāṇa) as the cosmic pillar.

###### Kauṣītaki Āraṇyaka (Śāṅkhāyana) — कौषीतकी आरण्यक (शाङ्खायन)

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Hotṛ (Forest Meditator) · **Structure:** 15 Chapters

Focuses on the internal fire sacrifice (Antar-Agnihotra), where the breath and mind act as the offerings and the self is the fire.

##### Āraṇyaka teachings

###### Prāṇa as Cosmic Support

**Citation:** AA 2.1.8

Sage Mahidasa Aitareya instructs that the vital breath (Prana) is the internal support of the body, just as the sun supports the sky.

> प्राणो वै शरीरस्य स्तम्भः ।
> यो वै प्राणं ब्रह्मेति वेदास्य देवा वशमायन्ति ॥
> “prāṇo vai śarīrasya stambhaḥ |
yo vai prāṇaṃ brahmeti vedāsya devā vaśamāyanti ||” — AA 2.1.8

Focuses on the creation of the cosmos from the absolute Self. Contains the famous Mahāvākya "Prajñānaṃ Brahma" (Consciousness is Brahman).

###### Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad — कौषीतकी उपनिषद्

##### Upaniṣad teachings

###### Prajñānaṃ Brahma

**Citation:** Aitareya Upaniṣad 3.1.3

Consciousness is Brahman. Declares that the supreme intelligence animating the universe is the ultimate reality.

> एष ब्रह्मा एष इन्द्र एष प्रजापतिरेते सर्वे देवाः ।
> प्रज्ञाननेत्रो लोकः प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठा प्रज्ञानं ब्रह्म ॥
> “eṣa brahmā eṣa indra eṣa prajāpatirete sarve devāḥ |
prajñānetro lokaḥ prajñā pratiṣṭhā prañjānaṃ brahma ||” — AU 3.1.3

#### Yajurveda

##### Śākhās (recensions)

###### Vājasaneyi Mādhyandina — वाजसनेयी माध्यन्दिन

**Category:** Śukla Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Structure:** 40 Adhyāyas (chapters), 1,975 Kandikās (verses) · **Region:** Dominant in North and Central India

Named after sage Yajnavalkya (Vajasaneya) and the midday sun. It is the most popular Shukla Yajurveda recension, recited with a distinctive pronunciation where 'ya' becomes 'ja' and 'va' becomes 'ba' in specific contexts.

###### Vājasaneyi Kāṇva — वाजसनेयी काण्व

**Category:** Śukla Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Structure:** 40 Adhyāyas, 328 Anuvākas, 2,086 verses · **Region:** Popular in South India, Maharashtra, and Odisha

Prevalent in South India. It is textually very close to Madhyandina but features minor word order variations and contains 111 additional verses. It serves as the basis for the Kanva Shatapatha Brahmana.

###### Taittirīya Saṃhitā — तैत्तिरीय संहिता

**Category:** Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Structure:** 7 Kāṇḍas, 44 Prapāṭhakas, 651 Anuvākas · **Region:** Dominant across South India

Named after sage Tittiri. The most widely chanted and preserved Black Yajurveda school, containing both mantras and prose commentary integrated together. Famous for its beautiful, resonant chanting flow.

###### Maitrāyaṇī Saṃhitā — मैत्रायणी संहिता

**Category:** Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Structure:** 4 Kāṇḍas, 54 Prapāṭhakas, 3,144 mantras · **Region:** Preserved in Western Maharashtra and Gujarat

Named after sage Maitri. It preserves archaic linguistic features and unique ritual details. Today, a small community of priests in Nashik and Nandurbar (Maharashtra) maintains its oral chanting.

###### Kāṭhaka / Kaṭha Saṃhitā — काठक / कठ संहिता

**Category:** Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Structure:** 5 Books, 40 Chapters · **Region:** Historically from Kashmir & Punjab

Originally flourished in Kashmir. Textually preserves ancient forms of prose commentary, containing teachings referenced by Pāṇini.

###### Kapiṣṭhala-Kaṭha — कपिष्ठल-कठ संहिता

**Category:** Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · **Status:** Partially Extant · **Structure:** Fragmentary books · **Region:** Historically Punjab region

A closely related branch to the Katha school, surviving only in fragmented manuscripts. It retains very archaic accentuation systems.

##### Core teachings

###### Śrī Rudram (Namakam)

**Citation:** TS 4.5 / VS 16

A grand Vedic hymn addressed to Rudra (the transformative cosmic aspect, Shiva), recognizing his presence in both benign and fierce forces of nature.

> नमस्ते रुद्र मन्यव उतो त इषवे नमः ।
> नमस्ते अस्तु धन्वने बाहुभ्यामुत ते नमः ॥
> “namaste rudra manyava uto ta iṣave namaḥ |
namaste astu dhanvane bāhubhyāmuta te namaḥ ||” — TS 4.5.1.1

###### Śivasaiṅkalpa Sūkta

**Citation:** VS 34.1-6

A beautiful prayer asking that the mind—which wanders far during waking and sleep, and is the source of all action—be filled with auspicious, noble thoughts.

> यज्जाग्रतो दूरमुदैति दैवं तदु सुप्तस्य तथैवैति ।
> दूरङ्गमं ज्योतिषां ज्योतिरेकं तन्मे मनः शिवसङ्कल्पमस्तु ॥
> “yajjāgrato dūramudaiti daivaṃ tadu suptasya tathaivaiti |
dūraṅgamaṃ jyotiṣāṃ jyotirekaṃ tanme manaḥ śivasaṅkalpamastu ||” — VS 34.1

###### Mahāmṛtyuñjaya Mantra

**Citation:** TS 1.8.6 / RV 7.59.12

A powerful mantra seeking liberation from death and decay, comparing spiritual liberation to a cucumber detaching effortlessly from its vine.

> त्र्यम्बकं यजामहे सुगन्धिं पुष्टिवर्धनम् ।
> उर्वारुकमिव बन्धनान्मृत्योर्मुक्षीय मामृतात् ॥
> “tryambakaṃ yajāmahe sugandhiṃ puṣṭivardhanam |
urvārukamiva bandhanānmṛtyormukṣīya māmṛtāt ||” — TS 1.8.6.2

###### Śānti Mantra (Shanti)

**Citation:** VS 36.17

A grand invocation of peace for the entire cosmos, praying for tranquility in the sky, earth, waters, plants, trees, and the ultimate Brahman.

> द्यौः शान्तिरन्तरिक्षं शान्तिः पृथिवी शान्तिरापः शान्तिरोषधयः शान्तिः ।
> वनस्पतयः शान्तिर्विश्वेदेवाः शान्तिर्ब्रह्म शान्तिः सर्वं शान्तिः शान्तिरेव शान्तिः सा मा शान्तिरेधि ॥
> “dyauḥ śāntirantarikṣaṃ śāntiḥ pṛthivī śāntirāpaḥ śāntiroṣadhayaḥ śāntih |
vanaspatayaḥ śāntirviśvedevāḥ śāntirbrahma śāntiḥ sarvaṃ śāntih śāntireva śāntih sā mā śāntiredhi ||” — VS 36.17

##### Brāhmaṇas

###### Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa — शतपथ ब्राह्मण

**Category:** Śukla Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant (Mādhyandina & Kāṇva) · **Role:** Adhvaryu (Officiant) · **Structure:** 100 Chapters (Mādhyandina) / 104 (Kāṇva)

The largest prose work of the Vedic canon (meaning "One Hundred Paths"). Details the geometry of altars, agricultural rites, early astronomy, and historic legends like Manu and the Flood.

###### Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa — तैत्तिरीय ब्राह्मण

**Category:** Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Adhvaryu (Officiant) · **Structure:** 3 Books (Ashtakas)

Contains explanations of domestic and community rituals, the cosmic significance of sacrificial timings, and details the astronomical constellations (Nakshatras) and early dialogues of Nachiketas.

##### Brāhmaṇa teachings

###### Manu and the Flood

**Citation:** SB 1.8.1

The earliest Indic account of a global deluge. Manu shelters a tiny fish that grows to guide his ship to safety amidst the rising waters.

> मनवे ह वै प्रातर्हस्तेऽवनेग्यमुदकमाहरन् ।
> तस्य ह हस्त आवनेग्यमुदकमानिन्युस्तत्र मत्स्यः समाबभूव ॥
> “manave ha vai prātahaste'vanēgyamudakamāharan |
tasya ha hasta āvanēgyamudakamāninyustatra matsyaḥ samābabhūva ||” — SB 1.8.1.1

###### Purūravas and Urvaśī

**Citation:** SB 11.5.1

The poignant love story of mortal king Purūravas and celestial nymph Urvaśī. One of the oldest surviving dramatic narratives in world literature, later retold by Kālidāsa.

> उर्वशी ह वै अप्सरा पुरूरवसमैडमभिवव्राज ।
> सा हास्मै प्रतिजज्ञे ॥
> “urvaśī ha vai apsarā purūravasamaiḍamabhivvavrāja |
sā hāsmai pratijajñe ||” — SB 11.5.1.1

###### The Agnicayana (Fire Altar)

**Citation:** SB 6.1-9.5

The monumental description of constructing the great fire altar in the shape of a cosmic eagle (Śyena). Encodes advanced geometry including early forms of the Pythagorean theorem.

> प्रजापतिर्वा इदमग्र एक एवास ।
> सोऽकामयत बहु स्यां प्रजायेयेति ।
> स तपोऽतप्यत ॥
> “prajāpatirvā idamagra eka evāsa |
so'kāmayata bahu syāṃ prajāyeyeti |
sa tapo'tapyata ||” — SB 6.1.1.1

###### Prajāpati's Self-Creation

**Citation:** SB 6.1.1

Prajāpati (the creator) produces the universe through intense austerity (Tapas). After creation, he feels exhausted and disintegrated, and the ritual fire altar reconstructs him — a powerful metaphor for cosmic renewal.

> प्रजापतिरकामयत प्रजायेयेति ।
> स तपोऽतप्यत ।
> स तपस्तप्त्वा इदं सर्वमसृजत यदिदं किं च ॥
> “prajāpatir akāmayata prajāyeyeti |
sa tapo'tapyata |
sa tapas taptvā idaṃ sarvam asṛjata yad idaṃ kiṃ ca ||” — SB 6.1.1.1

###### Nachiketas's First Appearance

**Citation:** TB 3.11.8

The earliest reference to the Nachiketas legend. A young boy, given to Death by his father's rash vow, becomes the prototype of the fearless seeker of truth — later elaborated in the Kaṭha Upaniṣad.

> नचिकेता ह वै गौतमः पितरं प्रोवाच ।
> तत कस्मै मां दास्यतीति ।
> मृत्यवे त्वा ददामीति ॥
> “naciketā ha vai gautamaḥ pitaraṃ provāca |
tat kasmai māṃ dāsyatīti |
mṛtyave tvā dadāmīti ||” — TB 3.11.8.1

##### Āraṇyakas

###### Bṛhadāraṇyaka — बृहदारण्यक

**Category:** Śukla Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Adhvaryu (Forest Meditator) · **Structure:** 6 Chapters

The "Great Forest Text" found at the close of the Satapatha Brahmana. Interprets the horse sacrifice cosmically and introduces early concepts of karma and transmigration.

###### Taittirīya Āraṇyaka — तैत्तिरीय आरण्यक

**Category:** Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Adhvaryu (Forest Meditator) · **Structure:** 10 Chapters (Prapathakas)

Contains details of internal sacrifices, rules of Yajñopavīta (sacred thread), daily prayers (Sandhyāvandana), and the famous Mahānārāyaṇa portion which praises the supreme soul.

##### Āraṇyaka teachings

###### Macrocosmic Horse

**Citation:** BA 1.1

A meditation where the sacrificial horse is seen as the cosmos itself: dawn is its head, wind is its breath, and the stars are its body.

> उषा वा अश्वस्य मेध्यस्य शिरः ।
> सूर्यश्चक्षुर्वातः प्राणो व्यात्तमग्निवैश्वानरः ॥
> “uṣā vā aśvasya medhyasya śiraḥ |
sūryāścakṣurvātaḥ prāṇo vyāttamagnirvaiśvānaraḥ ||” — BA 1.1.1

##### Upaniṣads of this Veda

###### Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad — बृहदारण्यक उपनिषद्

**Category:** Śukla Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Adhvaryu (Vedānta Sage) · **Structure:** 6 Chapters

The largest and oldest Upanishad, famous for dialogues of Sage Yajnavalkya with Maitreyi and Gargi on the nature of the self. Contains the Mahāvākya "Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi" (I am Brahman).

###### Īśa Upaniṣad — ईश उपनिषद्

**Category:** Śukla Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Adhvaryu (Vedānta Sage) · **Structure:** 18 Verses

The final chapter of Shukla Yajurveda Samhita. Teaches that the entire universe is pervaded by the Divine, and advocates selfless action.

###### Taittirīya Upaniṣad — तैत्तिरीय उपनिषद्

**Category:** Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Adhvaryu (Vedānta Sage) · **Structure:** 3 Parts (Śikṣā, Brahmānanda, Bhṛgu Vallī)

Contains instructions on ethics, the famous definition of Brahman as Truth-Knowledge-Infinity, and the theory of five Koshas (sheaths) of human existence.

###### Kaṭha Upaniṣad — कठ उपनिषद्

**Category:** Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · **Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Adhvaryu (Vedānta Sage) · **Structure:** 2 Chapters, 3 Vallis each

The famous dialogue between young Naciketas and Yama (Death) on the nature of the soul and what lies beyond death.

##### Upaniṣad teachings

###### Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi

**Citation:** Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.10

I am Brahman. Realization of the absolute unity between the individual self (Atman) and the universal self.

> ब्रह्म वा इदमग्र आसीत् तदात्मानमेवावेत् ।
> अहं ब्रह्मास्मीति तस्मात्सर्वमभवत् ॥
> “brahma vā idamagra āsīt tadātmānamevāvet |
ahaṃ brahmāsmīti tasmātsarvamabhavat ||” — BU 1.4.10

#### Sāmaveda

##### Śākhās (recensions)

###### Kauthuma Saṃhitā — कौथुम संहिता

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Region:** Gujarat, Maharashtra, and parts of Tamil Nadu

The most popular and widely studied recension of the Sāmaveda, dividing chants into Purvarcika and Uttararcika.

###### Rāṇāyanīya Saṃhitā — राणायनीय संहिता

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Structure:** Divided into Purvarcika & Uttararcika, similar to Kauthuma · **Region:** Karnataka, Maharashtra, and Odisha

Prevalent in Karnataka, Maharashtra, and Odisha. It is textually very close to the Kauthuma branch but differs slightly in chanting style and pronunciation.

###### Jaiminīya / Talavakāra — जैमिनीय / तलवकार

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Structure:** Distinct musical notation system · **Region:** Kerala and Tamil Nadu

An ancient and highly distinct recension, preserved primarily in Kerala and Tamil Nadu. Its musical notation and chanting system are older and differ significantly from the Kauthuma branch.

##### Core teachings

###### Bṛhat Sāma

**Citation:** SV Gāna Tradition

A magnificent melody sung to express cosmic grandeur and strength. In the Bhagavad Gita (10.35), Lord Krishna declares: "Of hymns, I am the Bṛhat-sāma."

> वेदानां सामवेदोऽस्मि देवानामस्मि वासवः ।
> इन्द्रियाणां मनश्चास्मि भूतानामस्मि चेतना ॥
> “vedānāṃ sāmavedo'smi devānāmasmi vāsavaḥ |
indriyāṇāṃ manaścāsmi bhūtānāmasmi cetanā ||” — Bhagavad Gītā 10.22

###### Rathantara Sāma

**Citation:** SV 1.1.1

One of the oldest and most sacred melodies of the Sāmaveda, traditionally associated with the cosmic order (Ṛta) and the movement of the sun.

> अग्न आ याहि वीतये गृणानो हव्यदातये ।
> नि होता सत्सि बर्हिषि ॥
> “agna ā yāhi vītaye gṛṇāno havyadātaye |
ni hotā satsi barhiṣi ||” — SV 1.1.1

###### Vāmadevya Sāma

**Citation:** SV 1.169

A beautiful melody chanted to invoke absolute harmony, natural growth, and peace, symbolizing the vital breath (Prana) and cosmic integration.

> कया नश्चित्र आ भुवदूती सदावृधः सखा ।
> कया शचिष्ठया वृता ॥
> “kayā naścitra ā bhuvadūtī sadāvṛdhaḥ sakhā |
kayā śaciṣṭhayā vṛtā ||” — SV 1.169

##### Brāhmaṇas

###### Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa (Tāṇḍya) — पञ्चविंश ब्राह्मण (ताण्ड्य)

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Chanter) · **Structure:** 25 Books

Focuses on Sāmavedic choral chants and melodies. Details purification rituals like Vrātyastoma to readmit those who have strayed from the Vedic lifestyle back into society.

###### Ṣaḍviṃśa Brāhmaṇa — षड्विंश ब्राह्मण

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Chanter) · **Structure:** 5 Chapters (serves as 26th book)

An appendix to the Pancavimsha. Its final part (Adbhuta Brahmana) is the earliest Indic text discussing unfavorable astrological signs, earthquakes, dreams, and their remedies.

###### Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa — जैमिनीय ब्राह्मण

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Chanter) · **Structure:** 3 Books

A vast depository of legends and stories about sages. Explains the origin, tuning, and metaphysical powers of specific musical pitches and Sāman melodies.

###### Sāmavidhāna Brāhmaṇa — सामविधान ब्राह्मण

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Chanter) · **Structure:** 3 Prapathakas

Prescribes which specific Sāman chants to employ for particular ritual and spiritual purposes. Acts as a practical manual linking melodies to their intended effects — from healing ailments to invoking rain.

###### Ārṣeya Brāhmaṇa — आर्षेय ब्राह्मण

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Chanter) · **Structure:** Catalog text

Catalogs the Ṛṣis (seers/composers) associated with each Sāman melody. Serves as a reference index linking every chant to its founding sage and providing the lineage of authorship.

###### Devatādhyāya Brāhmaṇa — देवताध्याय ब्राह्मण

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Chanter) · **Structure:** Catalog text

Lists the presiding deity (Devatā) for each Sāman chant. A companion text to the Ārṣeya Brāhmaṇa — while the latter identifies the seer, this text identifies the divine patron of each melody.

###### Vaṃśa Brāhmaṇa — वंश ब्राह्मण

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Chanter) · **Structure:** Single chapter

Preserves the guru-śiṣya paramparā (teacher-student lineage) of the Sāmaveda tradition. Considered the oldest surviving record of an Indian intellectual lineage, tracing the chain of transmission across generations.

###### Mantra Brāhmaṇa (Chāndogya) — मंत्र ब्राह्मण (छान्दोग्य)

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Chanter) · **Structure:** 2 Prapathakas

Explains the chants and mantras used in domestic ceremonies — marriage (vivāha), funeral (antyeṣṭi), and other sacraments (saṃskāras). Bridges the gap between Vedic ritual and household life.

###### Saṃhitopaniṣad Brāhmaṇa — संहितोपनिषद् ब्राह्मण

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Chanter) · **Structure:** 5 Chapters

Deals with the phonetic rules of combining (sandhi) Sāman syllables during continuous chanting. Treats the science of phonetics (śikṣā) as a spiritual discipline, uniting sound and meditation.

##### Brāhmaṇa teachings

###### Melodic Conquest

**Citation:** PB 6.1

Explains how the devas vanquished chaotic forces not with physical armor, but by matching the musical notes and frequencies of the Sāmans.

> देवा वै सामभिः सर्वांल्लभन्त ।
> तस्मात् सामगानं परमं साधनमुच्यते ॥
> “devā vai sāmabhiḥ sarvāṃllabhanta |
tasmāt sāmagānaṃ paramaṃ sādhanamucyate ||” — PB 6.1.2

###### Vrātyastoma (Readmission Rite)

**Citation:** PB 17.1-4

The remarkable ritual for readmitting Vrātyas (those who had fallen outside the Vedic fold) back into society. One of the earliest concepts of social reintegration and redemption in world literature.

> व्रात्यस्तोमेन वै देवा व्रात्यानभ्यवर्तयन् ।
> ते पुनर्वेदमविशन् ॥
> “vrātyastomena vai devā vrātyān abhyavartayan |
te punar vedam aviśan ||” — PB 17.1.2

###### Adbhuta (Ominous Portents)

**Citation:** ŚB 5.1-5

The Ṣaḍviṃśa Brāhmaṇa's fascinating final section (Adbhuta Brāhmaṇa) cataloging earthquakes, eclipses, unusual animal behavior, and dreams as cosmic signals, with prescribed Śānti rituals to avert calamity.

> अथ यदद्भुतानि प्रादुर्भवन्ति ।
> तेषां शान्तिं कुर्वीत ॥
> “atha yad adbhutāni prādurbhavanti |
teṣāṃ śāntiṃ kurvīta ||” — ŚB 5.1

###### Origin of the Seven Musical Notes

**Citation:** JB 1.1.1

The Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa's account of how the seven musical notes (saptasvara) emerged from the cosmic sacrifice, linking the Indian musical octave to Vedic ritual cosmology.

> प्रजापतिर्यज्ञमसृजत ।
> तस्माद्वाक्सृष्टा सामाभवत् ।
> सामगानात् सप्तस्वराः प्रादुर्भवन् ॥
> “prajāpatir yajñam asṛjata |
tasmād vāk sṛṣṭā sāmābhavat |
sāmagānāt saptasvarāḥ prādurbhavan ||” — JB 1.1.1

##### Āraṇyakas

###### Chāndogya Āraṇyaka — छान्दोग्य आरण्यक

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Forest Meditator) · **Structure:** Forms initial part of Chandogya Upanishad

Details meditation on the sacred chant Om (Pranava) and links the musical pitch of Sāman singing to the planetary cycles and seasonal winds.

###### Talavakāra Āraṇyaka (Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa) — तलवकार आरण्यक (जैमिनीय उपनिषद ब्राह्मण)

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Forest Meditator) · **Structure:** 4 Chapters

Discusses the correspondences of speech, vital breath, and sound. It transitions directly into the famous Kena Upanishad.

##### Āraṇyaka teachings

###### Om as Immortal Sound

**Citation:** TA 1.1 (JUB)

Meditation on Om as the source of all human speech and the immortal essence of cosmic energy.

> ओमित्येतदक्षरमुद्गीथमुपासीत ।
> तस्योपव्याख्यानं सर्वं हि वाङ् मयम् ॥
> “omityetadakṣaramudgīthamupāsīta |
tasyopavyākhyānaṃ sarvaṃ hi vāṅ mayam ||” — TA 1.1.1

##### Upaniṣads of this Veda

###### Chāndogya Upaniṣad — छान्दोग्य उपनिषद्

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Vedānta Sage) · **Structure:** 8 Chapters

One of the most extensive Upanishads, containing the Mahāvākya "Tat Tvam Asi" (That thou art) and teachings on Om and sacrifice.

###### Kena Upaniṣad — केनोपनिषद्

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Udgātṛ (Vedānta Sage) · **Structure:** 4 Sections

Investigates the unseen power behind the mind, senses, and breath. Famous for the parable of gods' pride broken by a Yaksha.

##### Upaniṣad teachings

###### Tat Tvam Asi

**Citation:** Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7

That thou art. Sage Uddālaka teaches Śvetaketu that he is identical to the subtle reality that pervades the cosmos.

> स य एषोऽणिमैतदात्म्यमिदं सर्वं तत्सत्यं स आत्मा ।
> तत्त्वमसि श्वेतकेतो इति ॥
> “sa ya eṣo'ṇimaitadātmyamidaṃ sarvaṃ tatsatyaṃ sa ātmā |
tat tvam asi śvetaketo iti ||” — CU 6.8.7

#### Atharvaveda

##### Śākhās (recensions)

###### Śaunakīya Saṃhitā — शौनकीय संहिता

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Structure:** 20 Kāṇḍas (books), 730 Hymns, ~6,000 verses · **Region:** Prevalent across India

The standard, most popular, and fully preserved recension of the Atharvaveda, comprising 20 books (kāṇḍas) containing 730 hymns.

###### Paippalāda Saṃhitā — पैप्पलाद संहिता

**Status:** Fully Extant / Recently Restored · **Structure:** 20 Kāṇḍas, different order and additional hymns · **Region:** Odisha and parts of Kashmir

An older recension containing different arrangements and additional hymns. It was rediscovered in Kashmir and is actively preserved in Odisha.

##### Core teachings

###### Bhūmi Sūkta (Pṛthivī Sūkta)

**Citation:** AV 12.1

A magnificent 63-verse hymn to Mother Earth, celebrated as the world's earliest anthem of ecological consciousness and harmony between humans and nature.

> शिला भूमिरश्मा पांसुः सा भूमिः संधृता मयू ।
> तस्यै  हिरण्यवक्षसे पृथिव्या अकरं नमः ॥
> माता भूमिः पुत्रो अहं पृथिव्याः ॥
> “śilā bhūmi raśmā pāṃsuḥ sā bhūmiḥ saṃdhṛtā mayū |
tasyai hiraṇyavakṣase pṛthivyā akaraṃ namaḥ ||
mātā bhūmiḥ putro ahaṃ pṛthivyāḥ ||” — AV 12.1.26 / 12.1.12

###### Prāṇa Sūkta

**Citation:** AV 11.4

A profound hymn glorifying Prāṇa (the vital breath/life-force) as the supreme ruler of all beings, which animates the universe.

> प्राणाय नमो यस्य सर्वमिदं वशे ।
> यो भूतः सर्वस्येश्वरो यस्मिन् सर्वं प्रतिष्ठितम् ॥
> “prāṇāya namo yasya sarvamidaṃ vaśe |
yo bhūtaḥ sarvasyeśvaro yasmin sarvaṃ pratiṣṭhitam ||” — AV 11.4.1

###### Kāla Sūkta

**Citation:** AV 19.53-54

A profound philosophical hymn that personifies Time (Kāla) as the primordial horse, the generator of heaven and earth, and the supreme ruler of all.

> कालो अश्वो वहति सप्तरश्मिः सहस्राक्षो अजरो भूरिरेताः ।
> तमारोहन्ति कवयो विपश्चितस्तस्य चक्रा भुवनानि विश्वा ॥
> “kālo aśvo vahati saptaraśmiḥ sahasrākṣo ajaro bhūriretāḥ |
tamārohanti kavayo vipaścitastasya cakrā bhuvanāni viśvā ||” — AV 19.53.1

##### Brāhmaṇas

###### Gopatha Brāhmaṇa — गोपथ ब्राह्मण

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Brahman (Overseer) · **Structure:** 2 Parts (Pūrva & Uttara)

The only surviving Brāhmaṇa of the Atharvaveda. Focuses on the role of the supervising Brahman priest who checks and corrects ritual errors. Explains the mystical meaning of the Om syllable and the creation of speech.

##### Brāhmaṇa teachings

###### Gayatri as Vedic Mother

**Citation:** GB 1.1.38

Exalts the Gayatri mantra as the absolute source from which the four Vedas emerged, framing it as the starting point of wisdom.

> ओङ्कारं पृच्छामः कोऽयं धातुः किं प्रातिपदिकं किमक्षरम् ।
> तस्माद् गायत्री वेदानां माता संबभूव ॥
> “oṅkāraṃ pṛcchāmaḥ ko'yaṃ dhātuḥ kiṃ prātipadikaṃ kimakṣaram |
tasmād gāyatrī vedānāṃ mātā saṃbabhūva ||” — GB 1.1.38

###### The Brahman Priest's Vigil

**Citation:** GB 1.2.1-18

Explains the unique role of the Brahman priest — the silent fourth officiant who oversees the entire sacrifice without performing any active role, but who corrects errors through mental mantras.

> ब्रह्मा वाव यज्ञस्य दक्षिणतो निषीदति ।
> स मनसा यज्ञमभिरक्षति ॥
> “brahmā vāva yajñasya dakṣiṇato niṣīdati |
sa manasā yajñam abhirakṣati ||” — GB 1.2.1

###### Praṇava (Om) as Seed of Creation

**Citation:** GB 1.1.1-25

The Gopatha Brāhmaṇa's teaching that the syllable Om preceded all creation and is the acoustic seed from which the four Vedas, all speech, and the entire cosmos emanated.

> ओमित्येतदक्षरमिदं सर्वम् ।
> तस्माद्ओमिति ब्राह्मणः प्रवक्ष्यन्नाह ।
> ओमित्याश्रावयति ॥
> “om ity etad akṣaram idaṃ sarvam |
tasmād om iti brāhmaṇaḥ pravakṣyann āha |
om ity āśrāvayati ||” — GB 1.1.1

##### Āraṇyakas

###### No Independent Āraṇyaka — स्वतंत्र आरण्यक नाही

**Status:** No Surviving Text · **Structure:** N/A

The Atharvaveda has no surviving independent Āraṇyaka text. Its transition from ritual directly to philosophy is preserved in the Gopatha Brāhmaṇa.

##### Āraṇyaka teachings

###### Transition to Wisdom

**Citation:** AV Tradition

In the absence of an Aranyaka, the Atharvaveda emphasizes that physical actions are merely preparation for inner wisdom (Tapas).

> तपसा चीयते ब्रह्म ततोऽन्नमभिजायते ।
> अन्नात् प्राणो मनः सत्यं लोकाः कर्मसु चामृतम् ॥
> “tapasā cīyate brahma tato'nnamabhijāyate |
annāt prāṇo mana's satyaṃ lokāḥ karmasu cāmṛtam ||” — Mundaka 1.1.8

##### Upaniṣads of this Veda

###### Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad — मुण्डक उपनिषद्

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Brahman (Vedānta Sage) · **Structure:** 3 Mundakas, 2 Khandas each

Distinguishes lower from higher knowledge. Contains the national motto "Satyameva Jayate" (Truth alone triumphs) and the metaphor of two birds.

###### Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad — माण्डूक्य उपनिषद्

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Brahman (Vedānta Sage) · **Structure:** 12 Verses

Shortest major Upanishad; maps out the four states of consciousness (waking, dreaming, sleeping, Turīya) to A-U-M. Contains "Ayam Ātmā Brahma".

###### Praśna Upaniṣad — प्रश्न उपनिषद्

**Status:** Fully Extant · **Role:** Brahman (Vedānta Sage) · **Structure:** 6 Questions

Six seekers ask Sage Pippalāda six fundamental questions about the source of creation, life-force (Prana), waking, dreaming, and the nature of consciousness.

##### Upaniṣad teachings

###### Ayam Ātmā Brahma

**Citation:** Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad 1.2

This Self is Brahman. Declares that the individual soul residing in all beings is identical to the infinite Brahman.

> सर्वं ह्येतद् ब्रह्म अयमात्मा ब्रह्म ।
> सोऽयमात्मा चतुष्पात् ॥
> “sarvaṃ hyetad brahma ayamātmā brahma |
so'yamātmā catuṣpāt ||” — MU 1.2

## 02. The Upavedas — उपवेदाः
`upavedas` · Applied · applied · 4 sciences

Ayurveda (life), Dhanurveda (arms), Gāndharvaveda (music), Sthāpatyaveda (architecture) — applied counterparts to each principal Veda.

*Vedic period · Practical sciences · Living disciplines*

### Detail content

#### Āyurveda — आयुर्वेद

**Veda Pair:** Atharvaveda · **Texts:** Caraka-saṃhitā, Suśruta-saṃhitā, Aṣṭāṅga-hṛdaya

> हिताहितं सुखं दुःखमायुस्तस्य हिताहितम् ।
> मानं च तच्च यत्रोक्तमायुर्वेदः स उच्यते ॥
> *hitāhitaṃ sukhaṃ duḥkhamāyustasya hitāhitam | mānaṃ ca tacca yatroktamāyurvedaḥ sa ucyate ||*
> “That science is called Āyurveda which describes what is good and bad for life, what is happy and unhappy, and provides the measure and guidelines for a wholesome lifespan.” — Caraka-saṃhitā · Sūtrasthāna 1.41

Āyurveda, the "science of longevity," is the Upaveda of the Atharvaveda. It treats the human being as an integrated whole of body, mind, and spirit, proposing that health is not merely the absence of disease but a dynamic state of physiological and psychological equilibrium.

The system operates around the balance of the Tridoṣas (biological humors) and recognizes that every individual possesses a distinct constitutional blueprint (Prakṛti). Disease arises when these humors fall out of balance due to improper diet, stress, seasonal shifts, or lifestyle. Treatment ranges from complex herbal pharmacology and dietary adjustments to Panchakarma (physical detoxification) and rejuvenation therapies.

##### Branches

- **Kāyacikitsā:** Internal medicine, dealing with the diagnosis and treatment of systemic body ailments.

- **Śalyatantra:** Surgery, detailing operational techniques, surgical instruments, and wound management.

- **Śālākyatantra:** Ophthalmology and ENT, focusing on diseases of the eyes, ears, nose, and throat.

- **Kaumārabhrtya:** Pediatrics, obstetrics, and gynecology, covering childcare and maternal health.

- **Agadatantra:** Toxicology, treating poisons from animals, plants, minerals, and artificial toxins.

- **Bhūtavidyā:** Psychiatry, treating mental disturbances through spiritual, herbal, and behavioral means.

- **Rasāyana:** Rejuvenation therapy, focus on longevity, memory improvement, and immune defense.

- **Vājīkaraṇa:** Virility and reproductive health, enhancing vitality and the health of offspring.

##### Concepts

- **Tridoṣa (Three Bio-Energies) (त्रिदोष):** The fundamental constitution of Vāta (air/ether), Pitta (fire/water), and Kapha (water/earth) that governs physiological processes.

- **Saptadhātu (Seven Tissues) (सप्तधातु):** The structural components of the body: Rasa (plasma), Rakta (blood), Māṃsa (muscle), Medas (fat), Asthi (bone), Majjā (marrow), and Śukra (reproductive).

- **Pañcakarma (Five Cleansings) (पञ्चकर्म):** The five therapeutic processes of detoxification: Vamana, Virecana, Basti, Nasya, and Raktamokṣaṇa.

- **Prakṛti (Individual Constitution) (प्रकृति):** The unique genetic and physical blueprint of an individual determined at the moment of conception.

#### Dhanurveda — धनुर्वेद

**Veda Pair:** Yajurveda · **Texts:** Vasiṣṭha Dhanurveda-saṃhitā, Agni Purāṇa (Warfare sections)

> चतुष्पादं धनुर्वेदं प्रवक्ष्याम्यनुपूर्वशः ।
> यस्य ज्ञानेन देवेन्द्रो विजयं समवाप्तवान् ॥
> *catuṣpādaṃ dhanurvedaṃ pravakṣyāmyanupūrvaśaḥ | yasya jñānena devendro vijayaṃ samavāptavān ||*
> “I shall now systematically expound the four-footed Dhanurveda, the supreme science through whose knowledge even the lord of heaven obtained victory.” — Vasiṣṭha Dhanurveda-saṃhitā · 1.1

Dhanurveda, the Upaveda of the Yajurveda, is the ancient Indian science of warfare, combat, and martial strategy. The name literally means "knowledge of the bow," highlighting the primary status that archery held in early Indian military discipline.

It encompasses the study of various weapons, physical conditioning, chariot and cavalry maneuvering, and complex army formations (Vyūha). Crucially, Dhanurveda is grounded in Yuddha-dharma—the moral code of war—which dictates that combat must be fought with honor, prohibiting the targeting of unarmed soldiers, surrendering foes, or using cruel and deceitful methods.

##### Branches

- **Mukta:** Thrown weapons, such as arrows, spears, and discuss/disks.

- **Amukta:** Unthrown weapons, including swords, maces, shields, and daggers.

- **Muktāmukta:** Weapons that can be thrown or held close, such as javelins and tridents.

- **Yantramukta:** Mechanical projectile devices, slings, and siege weaponry.

- **Bāhu-yuddha:** Unarmed close combat, wrestling, and pressure-point striking.

##### Concepts

- **Yuddha-dharma (Chivalry & Ethics) (युद्धधर्म):** The ethical code governing combat: protecting non-combatants, banning poisoned weapons, and ensuring fair matchups.

- **Dhanurvidyā (Sacred Archery) (धनुर्विद्या):** The discipline of archery, treated not just as a physical skill but as a deep meditative practice.

- **Marmasthāna (Vital Points) (मर्मस्थान):** The study of 107 vital energy spots on the body used for self-defense and martial healing.

#### Gāndharvaveda — गान्धर्ववेद

**Veda Pair:** Sāmaveda · **Texts:** Nāṭya-śāstra of Bharata Muni, Saṅgīta-ratnākara · **Focus:** Aesthetics, classical music, dance, dramaturgy, and theater

> गीतं वाद्यं च नृत्यं च त्रयं सङ्गीतमुच्यते ।
> मार्गदेशीविभागेन तद् द्वेधा परिकीर्तितम् ॥
> *gītaṃ vādyaṃ ca nṛtyaṃ ca trayaṃ saṅgītamucyate | mārgadeśīvibhāgena tad dvedhā parikīrtitam ||*
> “Vocal music, instrumental music, and dance are together called Saṅgīta. It is classified into two paths: Mārga (classical/sacred) and Deśī (regional/folk).” — Saṅgīta-ratnākara · 1.1.21

Gāndharvaveda, the Upaveda of the Sāmaveda, is the comprehensive study of music, dance, theater, and aesthetics. Rooted in the melodic chants of the Sāman priests, it views the arts not merely as entertainment, but as a path to spiritual absorption and liberation (Mokṣa).

The foundational authority, Bharata Muni's Nāṭya-śāstra, systematizes the rules of performance, defining the revolutionary Rasa theory. According to this theory, art succeeds when it evokes a refined, universal emotional state (Rasa) in the spectator, shifting them from temporary personal emotions into pure, transcendent experience.

##### Branches

- **Saṅgīta (Vocal Music):** The science of voice production, melodic cycles (Rāgas), and vocal techniques.

- **Vādya (Instrumental Music):** The study of musical instruments divided into stringed, wind, percussion, and solid categories.

- **Nṛtya & Nṛtta (Dance):** Expressive and pure physical dance, using complex footwork and mudras (hand gestures).

- **Nāṭya (Dramaturgy):** The art of theatrical production, stagecraft, scriptwriting, and performance.

##### Concepts

- **Rasa Theory (Aesthetics) (रस):** The eight (later nine) fundamental emotional responses evoked in the audience through artistic expression.

- **Saptasvara (Seven Notes) (सप्तस्वर):** The seven primary musical notes: Sa, Ri, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, and Ni, representing cosmic vibrations.

- **Tāla (Rhythmic Cycles) (ताल):** The structural time cycles and rhythmic beats that organize musical and dance performances.

#### Sthāpatyaveda — स्थापत्यवेद

**Veda Pair:** Ṛgveda · **Texts:** Mānasāra, Mayamatam, Samarāṅगाणा-sūtradhāra · **Focus:** Architecture, sacred geometry, town planning, and sculpture

> प्रासाद-शयन-यानानि मञ्चकादि यथाविधि ।
> वास्तुसंज्ञं गृहं तत्र वसतां सुखदायकम् ॥
> *prāsāda-śayana-yānāni mañcakādi yathāvidhi | vāstusaṃjñaṃ gṛhaṃ tatra vasatāṃ sukhadāyakam ||*
> “Temples, beds, vehicles, and thrones must be built in accordance with the sacred science. A home constructed according to Vāstu rules brings peace and happiness to those who dwell within.” — Mayamatam · 1.2

Sthāpatyaveda, the Upaveda of the Ṛgveda, is the science of architecture, engineering, civil planning, and iconography. It bridges physical utility with cosmic alignment, ensuring that human structures exist in harmony with natural currents and forces.

Central to this discipline is the Vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala—a grid diagram that represents the cosmic being bound to the earth. Houses, temples, and entire cities are laid out according to these grids, assigning specific rooms or public areas to elements (like water, fire, and wind) based on their cardinal directions to promote health and prosperity.

##### Branches

- **Vāstu-śāstra:** The design and alignment of houses, palaces, public buildings, and civil works.

- **Śilpa-śāstra:** Iconography, proportions of divine statues, and the art of rock sculpture.

- **Citrakalā:** Traditional painting, murals, and visual rendering on walls and canvases.

- **Bhūmi-lakṣaṇa:** Soil testing, site selection, topography, and hydrological surveying.

##### Concepts

- **Vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala (वास्तुपुरुषमण्डल):** The sacred grid plan representing the cosmic man, used to determine the energetic layout of buildings.

- **Ṣaḍāṅga (Six Proportions) (षडङ्ग):** The six mathematical ratios used to ensure structural strength, aesthetic balance, and cosmic alignment.

- **Sūtradhāra (Master Architect) (सूत्रधार):** The lead architect and engineer who holds the direct string of measurements and directs construction.

## 03. The Six Vedāṅgas — षड्वेदाङ्गानि
`vedangas` · Auxiliary · language · 6 limbs

The auxiliary disciplines that make the Veda intelligible — phonetics, ritual, grammar, etymology, prosody, astronomy.

*Śikṣā · Kalpa · Vyākaraṇa · Nirukta · Chandas · Jyotiṣa · Limbs of the Veda*

### Detail content

#### Śikṣā — शिक्षा

**Limb:** Mouth (मुखम्) · **Metaphor:** The organ of pronunciation and articulation · **Authority Text:** Pāṇinīya Śikṣā, Yājñavalkya Śikṣā · **Key Author:** Pāṇini, Yājñavalkya

> व्याघ्री यथा हरेत्पुत्रान्दंष्ट्राभ्यां न च पीडयेत् ।
> भीता पतनभेदाभ्यां तद्वद्वर्णान्प्रयोजयेत् ॥
> *vyāghrī yathā haret putrān daṃṣṭrābhyāṃ na ca pīḍayet | bhītā patana-bhedābhyāṃ tadvad varṇān prayojayet ||*
> “Just as a tigress carries her cubs in her mouth, holding them firmly with her teeth but never biting or hurting them, and ever fearful of dropping them, so should a chanter articulate every single syllable of the Veda — neither holding too tightly nor letting go too loosely.” — Pāṇinīya Śikṣā · 25

Śikṣā is the science of vocal pronunciation and phonetics. It is described as the "mouth" of the Veda because the Vedic hymns are meant to be heard and recited, not silently read. Precision in accentuation is considered crucial; a single mispronounced vowel or shifted accent could alter the entire meaning of a mantra and ruin the efficacy of a ritual.

Historically, Śikṣā texts created the most rigorous phonetic classifications of any ancient civilization. They mapped the exact places of articulation in the vocal tract and the types of effort required to produce consonants and vowels. This rigid preservation is the sole reason why the Vedas have survived in their original acoustic form for over three millennia without any dialectal drift.

##### Subdivisions

- **Varna (Letters):** The study of individual sounds, categorized into vowels (svara) and consonants (vyañjana).

- **Svara (Accents):** The three Vedic accents: Udātta (high-pitched), Anudātta (low-pitched), and Svarita (circumflex).

- **Mātrā (Quantity):** The duration of a sound, measured in Kalās or moments: short (hrasva), long (dīrgha), or prolated (pluta).

- **Bala (Articulation):** The force or effort of pronunciation, divided into internal and external articulatory efforts.

- **Sāma (Recitation):** The melody, rhythm, and uniformity of continuous chanting.

- **Santāna (Sandhi):** The phonetic rules governing the combination of words and syllables in continuous speech.

##### Key Concepts

- **Sthāna (Articulation Points) (स्थान):** The eight places where sounds are formed in the mouth and throat (Kanṭha, Tālu, Mūrdhā, Danta, Oṣṭha, Nāsikā, Urash, and Jihvāmūla).

- **Pratna (Effort) (प्रयत्न):** The muscular effort required for articulation: internal (ābhyantara) like contact, and external (bāhya) like aspiration.

- **Uccāraṇa (Recitation Rules) (उच्चारण):** Prescriptions for avoiding defects in chanting, such as reciting too fast, murmuring, or swallowing syllables.

#### Kalpa — कल्प

**Limb:** Arms (हस्तौ) · **Metaphor:** The organ of action and execution · **Authority Text:** Kalpasūtras (Śrauta, Gṛhya, Dharma, Śulba) · **Key Author:** Baudhāyana, Āpastamba, Āśvalāyana

> दीर्घचतुरस्रस्याक्ष्णया रज्जुः पार्श्वमानी तिर्यङ्मानी च यत्पृथग्भूते कुरुतस्तदुभयं करोतीति ॥
> *dīrghacaturasrasyākṣṇayā rajjuḥ pārśvamānī tiryaṅmānī ca yat pṛthagbhūte kurutastadubhayaṃ karotīti ||*
> “The diagonal rope of a rectangle produces both areas which the flank (long) side and the horizontal (short) side produce separately. (This is the earliest known formulation of what later became known as the Pythagorean theorem).” — Baudhāyana Śulba-sūtra · 1.48

Kalpa is the science of ritual application and conducts. It represents the "arms" of the Veda because it translates abstract Vedic sound into physical, structured action. It is written in the form of Sūtras (dense, aphoristic guidelines) that cover every aspect of a person's ritual, family, and civic duties.

The Kalpasūtras are categorized into four distinct layers. Among these, the Śulba-sūtras are of particular historical interest to science as they contain the oldest manuals for geometric construction of fire altars, embodying advanced mathematical principles (such as early formulations of the Pythagorean theorem) centuries before Greek geometry arose.

##### Subdivisions

- **Śrauta-sūtra:** Prescribes instructions for the grand, public sacrifices (Yajñas) involving the three sacred fires and multiple priests.

- **Gṛhya-sūtra:** Details domestic rituals and life-cycle ceremonies (Saṃskāras) such as birth, marriage, initiation (Upanayana), and funerals.

- **Dharma-sūtra:** Exposes code of ethics, social conduct, law codes, and duties (Dharma) of individuals at various life stages.

- **Śulba-sūtra:** The mathematical manuals prescribing geometry for designing, aligning, and building fire altars.

##### Key Concepts

- **Saṃskāra (Sacraments) (संस्कार):** The purificatory rites (usually 16) that mark transition phases of a human life from conception to cremation.

- **Yajña (Sacrifice) (यज्ञ):** Ritual offerings made into the fire to cosmic deities, serving as an expression of gratitude and maintaining cosmic order (Ṛta).

- **Vāstu (Sacred Space) (वास्तु):** Principles of layout and alignment of the sacrificial arena and altars to cosmic directions.

#### Vyākaraṇa — व्याकरण

**Limb:** Face/Mouth (मुखं व्याकरणं स्मृतम्) · **Metaphor:** The organ of formal speech and semantic structure · **Authority Text:** Aṣṭādhyāyī, Mahābhāṣya · **Key Author:** Pāṇini, Patañjali, Kātyāyana

> नृत्तावसाने नटराजराजो ननाद ढक्कां नवपञ्चवारम् ।
> उद्धर्तुकामः सनकादिसिद्धान्नेतद्विमर्शे शिवसूत्रजालम् ॥
> *nṛttāvasāne naṭarājarājo nanāda ḍhakkāṃ navapañcavāram | uddhartukāmaḥ sanakādisiddhān netadvimarśe śivasūtrajālam ||*
> “At the end of his cosmic dance, Śiva, the King of Dancers, sounded his drum fourteen times, desiring to uplift the sages Sanaka and others. Thus emerged the Shiva Sutras (fourteen sets of phonemes), serving as the foundational catalog of Sanskrit sounds for Panini.” — Nandikeśvara Kāśikā · 1

Vyākaraṇa is the grammar and linguistic analysis of Sanskrit. It is considered the "face" or "mouth" of the Veda because it clarifies the structural laws of speech. Pāṇini’s Aṣṭādhyāyī is the foundational text of this science, consisting of nearly 4,000 aphorisms (sūtras) that function like an algebraic algorithm or generative programming language to derive all classical and Vedic Sanskrit words from core roots and suffixes.

Grammarians within this tradition are revered as sages. The trio of Pāṇini, Kātyāyana, and Patañjali (known as the Munitraya) established a level of formal logic in language analysis that preceded modern generative linguistics by twenty-five centuries.

##### Subdivisions

- **Dhātupāṭha (Verb Roots):** The list of approximately 2,000 verbal roots (dhātus) from which all nouns and verbs are derived.

- **Gaṇapāṭha (Nominal Groups):** Groupings of nouns that share similar declensions or grammatical behaviors.

- **Śivasūtra (Phoneme Inventory):** The fourteen verses that organize sounds in a way that allows Pāṇini's rules to refer to groups of sounds concisely.

- **Aṣṭādhyāyī (Core Rules):** The eight chapters of generative rules describing phonetic shifts, word compounding, and case terminations.

##### Key Concepts

- **Prakṛti & Pratyaya (Base & Suffix) (प्रकृति-प्रत्यय):** The core linguistic methodology of splitting any word into its semantic root and its morphological suffix.

- **Sandhi (Phonetic Junction) (सन्धि):** Rules governing how sounds merge or shift when they come in contact with each other at word boundaries.

- **Kāraka (Case Relations) (कारक):** The semantic relation between the action (verb) and the participants (nouns) in a sentence, forming the basis of Sanskrit syntax.

#### Nirukta — निरुक्त

**Limb:** Ears (श्रोत्रम्) · **Metaphor:** The organ of hearing and semantic comprehension · **Authority Text:** Nirukta of Yāska · **Key Author:** Yāska

> चत्वारि पदजातानि नामाख्याते चोपसर्गनिपाताश्च ॥
> *catvāri padajātāni nāmākhyāte copasarganipātāśca ||*
> “There are four distinct classes of words: the nouns (nāma), the verbs (ākhyāta), the prepositions or prefixes (upasarga), and the particles or conjunctions (nipāta).” — Yāska · Nirukta · 1.1

Nirukta is the science of etymology and semantics. It represents the "ears" of the Veda because it is concerned with understanding the meaning of what is heard. It was written by the sage Yāska as a systematic commentary on the Nighaṇṭu — a glossary of rare, archaic, and difficult Vedic words.

Yāska's primary thesis was that all nouns are ultimately derived from verbal roots (dhātus). This semantic principle was a massive leap forward in lexicography, allowing interpreters to extract the deep inner meanings of Vedic hymns rather than relying on literal, superficial interpretations.

##### Subdivisions

- **Naighanṭuka Kānḍa:** The collection of synonymous words grouped together to clarify semantic fields.

- **Naigama Kānḍa:** A collection of difficult, homonymous words that occur in specific Vedic contexts.

- **Daivata Kānḍa:** The classification of deities and cosmic powers, mapped across the three regions of earth, atmosphere, and heaven.

##### Key Concepts

- **Padajāta (Parts of Speech) (पदजात):** The division of language into four distinct parts: Nāma (nouns), Ākhyāta (verbs), Upasarga (prefixes), and Nipāta (particles).

- **Nirvacana (Derivation) (निर्वाचन):** The etymological rules for tracing any noun back to its action root, even when phonetic shifts have obscured the connection.

- **Arthajñāna (Value of Meaning) (अर्थज्ञान):** The philosophical principle that reciting mantras without knowing their meaning is useless, comparing such a reciter to a pillar carrying weight.

#### Chandas — छन्दस्

**Limb:** Feet (पादौ) · **Metaphor:** The organ of support and movement · **Authority Text:** Chandaḥ-sūtra of Piṅgala · **Key Author:** Piṅgala · **Scope:** Poetic metres, syllable weight, rhythm, and combinatorics.

> सानुस्वारश्च दीर्घश्च विसर्गी च गुरुर्भवेत् ।
> वर्णो गुरुर्विज्ञेयः गद्यपद्येऽपि सर्वथा ॥
> *sānusvāraśca dīrghaśca visargī ca gururbhavet | varṇo gururvijñeyaḥ gadyapadye'pi sarvathā ||*
> “A syllable containing a short vowel followed by an anusvara, a long vowel, or followed by a visarga, is to be recognized as heavy (Guru) in both prose and verse.” — Piṅgala Chandaḥ-sūtra · Traditional metric rule

Chandas is the study of poetic metre. It is called the "feet" of the Veda because it forms the rhythmic support on which the hymns stand and move. Vedic literature is entirely poetic, and its chants are categorized strictly by the number of syllables per line.

Piṅgala's Chandaḥ-sūtra is the earliest known systematic treatment of this science. In his attempt to calculate all possible combinations of light and heavy syllables, Piṅgala laid down algebraic formulas that contain the earliest historical descriptions of binary numbers, combinatorial mathematics, and the "Meru-Prastāra" (which is now known in the West as Pascal's triangle).

##### Subdivisions

- **Vedic Metres:** Metres defined strictly by syllable count (Akṣara-vṛtta), dominated by the seven primary metres.

- **Classical Metres:** Later metres defined by the arrangement of light and heavy syllables (Mātrā-vṛtta).

- **Meru-Prastāra:** A geometric representation of combinations of syllables, equivalent to the binomial expansion triangle.

##### Key Concepts

- **Laghu & Guru (Syllable Weight) (लघु-गुरु):** The distinction between light/short (Laghu, 1 beat) and heavy/long (Guru, 2 beats) syllables based on vowel length and following clusters.

- **Gana (Metrical Feet) (गण):** Groups of three syllables used in classical metres, classified into eight standard types (Ya, Ma, Ta, Ra, Ja, Bha, Ja, Sa).

- **Prastāra (Permutation) (प्रस्तार):** The systematic methods for writing down and counting all possible metrical patterns of a given length.

#### Jyotiṣa — ज्योतिष

**Limb:** Eyes (नेत्रम्) · **Metaphor:** The organ of vision and cosmic alignment · **Authority Text:** Vedāṅga-jyotiṣa of Lagadha · **Key Author:** Lagadha

> यथा शिखा मयूराणां नागानां मणयो यथा ।
> तद्वद्वेदाङ्गशास्त्राणां गणितं मूर्धनि स्थितम् ॥
> *yathā śikhā mayūrāṇāṃ nāgānāṃ maṇayo yathā | tadvad vedāṅgaśāstrāṇāṃ gaṇitaṃ mūrdhani sthitam ||*
> “Just as the crest stands on the head of a peacock, and the jewel on the hood of a cobra, so does the science of mathematics (astronomy) stand at the very head of all the Vedāṅga auxiliary sciences.” — Lagadha · Vedāṅga-jyotiṣa · 35

Jyotiṣa is the science of astronomy and celestial timing. It represents the "eyes" of the Veda because it allows the tradition to see the cosmic time and perform sacrifices in alignment with celestial movements. Vedic rituals are tied strictly to solar and lunar cycles, solstices, and lunar mansions.

The earliest text, the Vedāṅga-jyotiṣa of sage Lagadha, lays out a luni-solar calendar of a five-year cycle (Yuga). It details the positions of the sun and moon relative to the twenty-seven Nakṣatras (lunar mansions). This mathematical discipline later evolved into the classical Indian astronomy of Aryabhata and Varahamihira.

##### Subdivisions

- **Siddhānta (Mathematical Astronomy):** Calculations of planetary positions, eclipses, and orbits.

- **Saṃhitā (Mundane Astrology):** The study of celestial events, earthquakes, and omen patterns on a collective scale.

- **Horā (Natal Astrology):** The study of natal charts and individual timings, which developed extensively in the classical post-Vedic period.

##### Key Concepts

- **Nakṣatra (Lunar Mansions) (नक्षत्र):** The twenty-seven segments of the ecliptic through which the moon passes during its sidereal orbit.

- **Ayana (Solstices) (अयन):** The six-month paths of the sun: Uttarāyaṇa (northward journey) and Dakṣiṇāyana (southward journey).

- **Muhūrta (Auspicious Moment) (मुहूर्त):** A unit of time (approximately 48 minutes) utilized to find the perfect moment to initiate any ritual or task.
